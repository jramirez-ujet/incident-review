EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The two weeks ending October 11 produced 12 incidents: 1 Critical, 2 Major and 9 Minor, our first Critical since August. Week one was quiet, with five incidents, all Minor. Everything severe landed in week two, and all three severe incidents were database failures. On Google CCaaS, Bell Canada went hard down and TTEC agents could not answer calls; both traced to undersized databases with missing indexes, and both were reported by the customer before our monitoring saw them. On the UJET platform, our own scheduled database-change tooling froze production six times during US business hours, failing roughly 28,000 requests across 21 tenants; that one our alerting caught. September 30 and October 8 each saw three incidents: the September 30 trio was unrelated, while October 8 carried both Majors. 2 P1 follow-ups remain open across the three severe incidents.

AFFECTED AREAS
GOOGLE CCAAS · Database / Custom Holidays
WHAT HAPPENED

Bell added custom holidays for Canadian Thanksgiving. The holiday hierarchy table was built in 2022 with no indexes, and an inefficient lookup ran a full scan of it for every holiday on every request. The new entries multiplied that load, the database hit 100% CPU, and Bell's entire contact center went down. Bell reported it; our monitoring did not catch it first.

HOW IT WAS RESOLVED

Deleting the new holidays cleared the pileup within about two hours and restored service. We then added the missing indexes to Bell production, saw query times drop immediately, and worked with Google and Bell to restore the holidays gradually while watching database load.

FOLLOW-UP ITEMS
Fix the holiday lookup so it stops scanning the table on every request (P1) (outstanding)
Add the missing indexes to a second table with the same gap, and block new tables shipping without them (outstanding)
Get Google to add database CPU alerting so we hear about this before the customer does (outstanding)

AFFECTED AREAS
GOOGLE CCAAS · Database / Agent Desktop
WHAT HAPPENED

TTEC's database was provisioned at the smallest size and hit 100% CPU, so agents could not answer calls. TTEC reported it. After we resized the database, agents still waited 15 to 40 seconds for calls to connect: pods were not scaling out fast enough under load, and the agent app was making the same requests repeatedly, amplifying the delay.

HOW IT WAS RESOLVED

We resized the database, which fixed the hard outage. The remaining slow connections were mitigated by moving TTEC to a larger instance tier and lowering the scaling threshold so pods scale out earlier. TTEC confirmed the improvement; we monitored through Oct 10 before resolving.

FOLLOW-UP ITEMS
Keep the lower scaling threshold from being silently reverted by the next deploy (outstanding)
Retune autoscaling so pods scale out before requests start queueing (outstanding)
Fix the agent app's repeated redundant requests during call assignment (outstanding)

AFFECTED AREAS
UJET · Database / Migration Tooling
WHAT HAPPENED

A scheduled database-change job rebuilt large tables tenant by tenant, and each final cleanup step froze the whole database for 17 to 46 seconds. Anything past about 15 seconds pulls servers out of the load balancer, so each freeze became a brief cluster-wide outage, during US business hours, with no one warned it would run. Our alerting caught it; the same pattern had appeared on Oct 2.

HOW IT WAS RESOLVED

We suspended the job across all production clusters and safely froze the two runs still in flight, avoiding two more and larger outages on Turo and KeyBank. The job stays off on the affected clusters until the change can be made without freezing the database. The honest caveat: the errors stopped because the job ran out of large tables, not because our mitigation landed first.

FOLLOW-UP ITEMS
Finish the safe cleanup of the two abandoned table copies in overnight windows (P1) (outstanding)
Move this job to off-peak windows per cluster, with the schedule published to deployers (outstanding)
Stop deploys from silently re-enabling the suspended job (outstanding)
plus 6 further outstanding follow-ups
