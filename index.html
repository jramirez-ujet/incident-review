EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

UJET logged 11 production incidents in the two weeks of July 27 through August 9: 4 Major (P1), no Critical, and 7 Minor. Six were on the UJET platform and five on Google CCaaS. The key risk signal is INC-453: an Authority host served MotivHealth and BCFS nothing for four and a half days with no alert firing. Its four open detection follow-ups, including one P0, are the most important items on this list.

WHAT HAPPENED

IIS on an Authority host (server28) stopped serving requests on July 23. MotivHealth and BCFS agents received errors on Authority call connectors for four and a half days. No alert fired: the host has no load balancer telemetry and its log shipper failed at the same time, so the outage stayed invisible until the customer reported it on July 27.

HOW IT WAS RESOLVED

An IIS restart restored service 18 minutes after declaration. The open follow-ups target the detection gap rather than the restart: liveness probes for all Authority hosts, restored log shipping, and the root cause of the IIS stop, which is still unknown.

FOLLOW-UP ITEMS
Restart the log-shipping agent on server28 (outstanding)
Add liveness / absent-traffic alerting for all Authority prod hosts (outstanding)
Collect server28 httperr and Windows event logs, determine the IIS trigger (outstanding)
plus 1 further open item on this incident

GOOGLE CCAAS · Voice / Dialogflow input
WHAT HAPPENED

After the welcome prompt, caller keypad and voice input stopped reaching the virtual agent on one CCaaS tenant, affecting about 80% of its calls; some calls hung until the caller gave up. Twilio traces showed no inbound audio arriving from the customer's carrier, placing the fault upstream of UJET and Google.

HOW IT WAS RESOLVED

The customer's carrier resolved the underlying telco issue, including number forwarding on their side. No change was needed on our platform, and the customer confirmed recovery the next day. The remaining follow-up fixes incident routing: this was first declared as UJET Prod, which paged the wrong on-call path.

FOLLOW-UP ITEMS
Improve incident declaration routing for CCAIP/CCaaS incidents (outstanding)

GOOGLE CCAAS · Agent status / backend
WHAT HAPPENED

About half of Equifax's agents could not change their status. Updates were completing, but only after roughly 51 seconds of server-side processing, so the interface appeared stuck or errored. Responders identified a tenant-specific billing job consuming backend capacity as the cause.

HOW IT WAS RESOLVED

The mitigation, suspending the tenant's billing cron and removing the running billing pod, was identified quickly but its execution was delayed by missing responder permissions, which extended recovery. Service returned to normal within an hour and a half. Follow-ups close the permissions gap and add a runbook.

FOLLOW-UP ITEMS
Fix incident responder permissions for billing job mitigation (outstanding)
Create a new runbook for incident skills (outstanding)

AFFECTED AREAS
GOOGLE CCAAS · Advanced Reporting / Looker
WHAT HAPPENED

Google's Looker 5.1 rollout shipped without required user attributes, so Advanced Reporting dashboards failed to render for multiple CCaaS tenants in us-east1 and US multi-region. Supervisors lost live queue monitoring. Google tracked this as a P0, and an initial rollback did not fix it.

HOW IT WAS RESOLVED

Adding the missing Looker user attributes restored dashboards, first validated with Intact and then applied across the remaining regional instances. Account teams confirmed recovery with impacted customers including Equifax. Follow-ups put Looker changes into release notes, add a deployment health check, and add Looker observability.

FOLLOW-UP ITEMS
Include Looker updates in the release notes (outstanding)
Add Looker health check to the deployment process (outstanding)
